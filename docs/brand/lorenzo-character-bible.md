## 1. Purpose

This document defines **Lorenzo the character** as the primary personification of the Lorenzo brand. Catileo is governed by the separate `catileo-character-bible.md`; this document references him only where his relationship with Lorenzo materially affects Lorenzo's characterization.

The brand identity document should define the visual system around them: logos, color tokens, typography, layout, product expression, and brand usage. This character bible should define the mascots themselves: who they are, how they look, how they move, how they speak, how they adapt across settings, and which traits must remain recognizable.

The two systems should support one another without collapsing into each other:

- **The spark is Lorenzo the brand.**
- **Lorenzo is the personification of the brand.**
- **Catileo is the secondary companion character and has his own character bible.**
- The mascot may be expressive, contextual, and narrative.
- The core logo system should remain simple, stable, and independent of mascot illustration.

## 2. Character at a glance

### Lorenzo

**Working archetype:** timeless androgynous cosmopolitan lorekeeper.

Other useful shorthand:

- gender-nonconforming celestial cartographer
- scholar-adventurer
- worldkeeper / archivist / navigator
- collector of places and stories
- researcher, writer, organizer, planner, guide
- clever host to strange worlds rather than heroic conqueror of them

Lorenzo is a charismatic scholar-adventurer with a polished, slightly roguish edge. They are curious, composed, perceptive, elegant, theatrical when they choose to be, and very comfortable around books, maps, data, stories, timelines, and places.

They are intellectually serious without being solemn. They enjoy research, organization, worldbuilding, and storytelling, but are equally at home traveling, improvising, getting lost, finding something unexpected, or spending an afternoon reading with Catileo nearby.

They are capable without presenting as physically dominant; stylish without feeling vain; luxurious without feeling impractical; clever without needing to prove it.

The emotional center of the character is:

> **A person you would happily spend an afternoon talking to about strange places and ideas.**

## 3. Core character principles

Every depiction of Lorenzo should preserve these principles.

### 3.1 Curious before heroic

Lorenzo notices, investigates, connects, questions, records, and guides.

They may take part in adventures, danger, exploration, or high drama, but the character's default function is not to dominate the situation. Their strength is attention.

### 3.2 Cultured, not pompous

Lorenzo is well-read, worldly, and articulate. They do not perform intelligence for status.

### 3.3 Elegant, not precious

They care about presentation, tailoring, materials, and details, but can climb, repair, fly, dive, hike, get dusty, or improvise when necessary.

### 3.4 Androgynous by design

Lorenzo should not resolve cleanly into a conventionally masculine or conventionally feminine presentation.

Different depictions may lean slightly more masc or femme, but the overall character remains recognizably gender-nonconforming and androgynous.

### 3.5 Approachable before imposing

Even at their most theatrical or grand, Lorenzo should remain human, conversational, and emotionally accessible.

### 3.6 Performance has an off-switch

Public Lorenzo is polished and composed.

Private Lorenzo slouches, mutters, reads in awkward positions, loses bookmarks, lets Catileo sleep on important notes, and becomes visibly less interested in maintaining a perfect silhouette.

This contrast is part of the character.

## 4. Canonicality model

Not every detail has the same importance.

### Tier A — identity anchors

These should survive almost every interpretation:

- asymmetric dark hair
- a shaved / tightly controlled right side
- blue concentrated in the longer swept hair, with blue strands, highlights and tips
- blue eyes
- expressive, slightly asymmetric eyebrows
- lean, elongated, androgynous silhouette
- characteristic relaxed asymmetry in posture
- a dark visual anchor in the clothing
- a recurring blue accent
- a restrained gold / navigation motif
- curiosity-driven behavior
- elegant hands and gestural body language
- they/them as default brand pronouns

### Tier B — strong recurring anchors

Usually present, but may translate with setting:

- compass / spark pendant or navigation medallion
- journal or equivalent recording device
- maps, charts, routes, diagrams, or linked information
- rings / earrings / subtle jewelry
- burgundy, teal, parchment, or warm neutrals
- blue ribbon / bookmark detail
- scholar-traveler visual language

### Tier C — context-dependent elements

Free to change:

- exact garments
- exact technology
- period styling
- shoes
- environment
- props beyond the core set
- hairstyle detailing below the main silhouette
- amount of decorative trim
- degree of realism
- amount of celestial imagery

## 5. Age and presentation

### Apparent age

Lorenzo is intentionally **age-ambiguous but unambiguously adult**.

A depiction might plausibly read as 25, 35, 45, or 50 depending on rendering, styling, lighting, and context.

The default impression should sit somewhere around mature young adult to middle-aged adult without strongly signaling a single decade.

Avoid:

- teen-coded proportions or behavior
- childlike facial simplification in normal character art
- making youthfulness the defining trait
- exaggerated aging markers unless a setting or story explicitly requires them

### Gender presentation

Lorenzo is always androgynous and gender-nonconforming.

Their presentation may move fluidly between masculine and feminine signals through:

- garment cut
- jewelry
- posture
- neckline
- tailoring
- makeup or lack thereof
- pose language
- hair styling
- footwear
- ornament

The goal is not "neutral by removing gendered detail."

The goal is **androgyny through deliberate combination**.

A successful depiction may include a masculine-cut coat with delicate jewelry, a soft blouse with broad structured shoulders, practical boots with elegant rings, or a theatrical silhouette paired with restrained makeup.

### Underlying body

The canonical body is lean and slightly top-heavy, with a fuller chest that is usually visually flattened through binding, layering, tailoring, or structured garments in standard depictions.

The chest should not become a focal point.

Avoid using anatomy to force the character into a strongly gendered read.

## 6. Proportions and silhouette

### 6.1 Full-body proportions

Lorenzo's proportions are stylized and fashion-illustration-like rather than strictly realistic.

Typical full-body read:

- roughly 7–7.5 heads tall
- relatively small torso
- long limbs
- especially long-looking neck and forearms
- moderately broad but non-bulky shoulders
- narrow ribcage and waist
- understated hips
- slightly oversized, elegant hands

The body should feel:

**lean + elongated + gestural + composed.**

rather than muscular, compact, or physically imposing.

### 6.2 Silhouette language

The default worldkeeper silhouette is:

**slender core + sharp shoulders + narrow waist + oversized sweeping outerwear + asymmetrical hair + active hands.**

The structured coat creates a strong upper frame, while wide sleeves and flaring coat tails broaden toward the bottom.

This produces an overall shape that transitions from a mild inverted triangle into an A-line.

### 6.3 Head silhouette

Hair is one of the strongest recognition features.

The silhouette should be:

- large on the long-hair side
- clean and tight on the shorter side
- visibly asymmetrical
- diagonally swept
- slightly rakish rather than perfectly groomed

The long side should carry the strongest blue accents.

### 6.4 Close-up proportion adjustment

At close range, slight exaggeration is acceptable:

- head can read larger
- visible torso can shorten
- shoulders can soften
- hand-to-face interactions may be emphasized
- expression takes precedence over fashion-illustration elongation

The character should still read as an adult.

## 7. Face and hair

### 7.1 Face

Key facial anchors:

- blue eyes
- expressive eyelids
- strong eyebrows
- one brow commonly sitting slightly higher than the other
- slightly asymmetric smile
- elegant but not severe jawline
- approachable resting expression
- head frequently held at a slight tilt

The eyebrows and eyelids should do more acting than the mouth.

Large mouth shapes should be reserved for genuinely large emotions.

### 7.2 Hair

Canonical hair:

- very dark brown / near-black base
- asymmetrical sidecut or undercut
- long swept mass over one side
- shorter or shaved opposite side
- blue concentrated in the long section
- blue most visible at tips, undersides, streaks, and selected forelock areas
- undercut side remains predominantly dark

The amount of visible blue may vary by illustration level and lighting.

The hair should **never become fully blue** in normal canon art.

#### Historical adaptation rule

The **shaved sidecut is canonical and remains literal in every genre and period**, including strict historical settings.

The rest of the hairstyle may be translated into period-appropriate dressing, texture, ornament, length, curling, braiding, pinning, or styling, but one side must still read as intentionally shaved / very closely clipped.

This is a deliberate anachronistic identity anchor. Do not "correct" it away for historical authenticity.

The result should still feel integrated into the setting: the sidecut stays Lorenzo; the longer hair adapts to the world.

## 9. Context-specific outfits

Lorenzo dresses appropriately for the task.

Examples:

- blue practical overalls for repair work
- pilot clothing for a biplane
- spacesuit for lunar exploration
- robes or formal mantle in a castle spire
- neoprene / diving suit underwater
- mountaineering layers while climbing
- lab / archive protective wear when required
- formal stagewear for presentation or performance

### Outfit translation rules

Every context-specific outfit should remain:

- comfortable
- pragmatic
- well-fitting
- slightly fashion-conscious
- androgynous
- coherent with the setting
- recognizably Lorenzo

Preserve at least three identity anchors where practical:

1. dark base or dark visual anchor
2. blue hair / blue accent
3. gold / navigation motif
4. asymmetrical silhouette
5. pendant / equivalent
6. elegant hand / accessory detail

Do not force burgundy, teal, parchment, and gold into every outfit.

## 11. Expression library

### Core expressions

These should cover most production needs.

1. **Neutral / Default**  
   Relaxed eyes, slight asymmetric smile, one brow subtly higher.

2. **Warm Smile**  
   Softened eyes, genuine closed-mouth smile, slightly lifted cheeks.

3. **Bright Delight**  
   Wider eyes, visible teeth, raised brows, open positive energy.

4. **Curious / Intrigued**  
   Head tilt, raised brow, lips faintly parted or pursed.

5. **Knowing Smirk**  
   Half-lidded eyes, one mouth corner raised.

6. **Focused / Analytical**  
   Slight brow draw, narrowed attention, neutral mouth.

7. **Awe / Wonder**  
   Widened eyes, relaxed brows, slightly open mouth.

8. **Skeptical**  
   One brow high, one low, mouth slightly pulled to one side.

9. **Deadpan / Unimpressed**  
   Half-lidded eyes, flat mouth, minimal facial movement.

10. **Concerned / Empathetic**  
    Inner brows raised, softened eyes, slight downturn to mouth.

11. **Determined**  
    Focused brows, chin slightly raised, firm mouth.

12. **Flustered / Caught Off Guard**  
    Uneven raised brows, widened eyes, compact mouth.

### Secondary expressions

1. Playfully mischievous
2. Mildly annoyed
3. Embarrassed but hiding it
4. Sad / reflective
5. Angry / protective
6. Exhausted / over-it
7. Laughing

### Expression rules

Across the library:

- eyebrows and eyelids do most of the acting
- mouth shapes are usually restrained
- one brow often sits higher
- smiles often remain asymmetric
- head tilts are common
- large expressions are more effective because they are relatively rare

## 13. Pose families by state

Think beyond "ways Lorenzo can stand."

The useful continuum is:

**public → conversational → working → resting → private → playful.**

### Close-up / head-and-shoulders

Useful motifs:

- chin on knuckles
- cheek in palm
- looking over a book
- adjusting earring / pendant
- brushing hair aside
- sideways knowing glance
- leaning toward camera as if sharing a secret
- looking upward out of frame
- temple against two fingers
- over-shoulder look

A recurring motif should be **face + hand interaction**.

### Bust / waist-up conversational

- book against chest + free-hand gesture
- loose crossed arms + pen
- elbow on surface
- leaning across desk
- partly turned away while speaking
- cup paused near mouth
- journal under arm
- two-handed explanation

### Sitting

- ankle over opposite knee
- sideways in chair
- arm over chair back
- desk annotation
- perched on table edge
- cross-legged on floor
- knees up while reading
- on steps with elbows on knees
- window seat
- backward on chair
- one leg tucked under

Almost never sit perfectly symmetrically.

### Lounging / relaxing

- book balanced on knee
- lying on stomach reading
- lying on back reading overhead
- curled into armchair
- head on folded arms at desk
- half-asleep over book
- stretched among maps
- reclining on one elbow
- blanket + cat
- leaning back with drink

### Desk / study

- map work
- journal writing
- pen tapping against lips
- moving book piles
- reaching for reference
- falling asleep on notes
- spinning compass
- examining artifact
- looking up when interrupted
- sitting back after solving something

### Floor-level

- cross-legged among books
- kneeling over unfolded map
- one knee raised while sketching

### Quiet / vulnerable

- forehead against window
- reading a letter

### Playful / candid

- laughing while nearly spilling drink

### Extreme close-up

- eye studying something
- fingers tracing map
- smirk detail
- hand around compass
- mouth partly hidden by book

- turning page
- profile lit by celestial object

## 17. Illustration simplification levels

Simplification changes production detail, not identity.

| Level | Name                   | Treatment                                                                 | Typical use                               |
| ----- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| L1    | Hero Illustration      | Full rendering, detailed anatomy, materials, lighting, props, environment | Key art, covers, landing pages, campaigns |
| L2    | Editorial Illustration | Cleaner shapes, reduced folds, flatter shading, fewer prop details        | Social banners, features, articles        |
| L3    | Character Emblem       | Strong silhouette, cel-shading, limited props, graphic motifs             | Brand badges, profiles, stickers          |
| L4    | Spot Illustration      | Bust / half-body, minimal shading, 0–2 props                              | UI states, cards, callouts                |
| L5    | Icon / Micro Lorenzo   | Head or symbolic silhouette, flat fills, minimal lines                    | Tiny avatars, reactions, micro-UI         |

### 17.1 Identity anchors at every level

Preserve:

- asymmetric dark hair
- blue sweep / highlights
- shorter right side
- blue eyes where visible
- dark/navy anchor
- gold compass / spark motif
- characteristic brow / smirk
- asymmetry in pose

### 17.2 Simplification is not chibi

The progression is:

**painted character → clean illustration → graphic character → spot character → symbol.**

Not:

**adult character → progressively childlike cartoon.**

Unless a non-canonical novelty asset explicitly calls for chibi treatment, do not use chibi proportions as the default simplification strategy.

### 17.3 Production complexity targets

| Feature          | L1        | L2                  | L3          | L4       | L5     |
| ---------------- | --------- | ------------------- | ----------- | -------- | ------ |
| Hair masses      | 20+       | 10–15               | 6–10        | 4–6      | 2–4    |
| Face shading     | Painterly | 3–5 tones           | 2–3 tones   | 1 shadow | Flat   |
| Clothing shading | Complex   | Moderate            | Simplified  | 1 shadow | Flat   |
| Props            | 4–8       | 3–6                 | 2–4         | 0–2      | 0      |
| Background       | Full      | Graphic environment | Emblem      | Motif    | None   |
| Line detail      | High      | Medium              | Clean       | Minimal  | Iconic |
| Texture          | High      | Low                 | Almost none | None     | None   |

## 18. Animation principles

The core motion rule is:

> **Eyes discover. Head interprets. Hands explain. Body follows. Clothing echoes.**

Lorenzo moves with deliberate elegance, then occasionally breaks that composure with quick flashes of curiosity, amusement, surprise, or embarrassment.

Stillness is part of the performance.

### 18.1 Motion profile

| Dimension         | Rule                         |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Default energy    | Calm, alert, self-possessed  |
| Movement size     | Small–medium                 |
| Movement speed    | Smooth and measured          |
| Reaction speed    | Fast when curiosity triggers |
| Symmetry          | Rare                         |
| Center of gravity | Shifted / relaxed            |
| Hands             | Highly expressive            |
| Head              | Frequent small tilts / turns |
| Eyes              | Often lead                   |
| Secondary motion  | Subtle hair / coat / jewelry |
| Exaggeration      | Moderate                     |
| Stillness         | Important                    |

### 18.2 Basic reaction rhythm

Typical sequence:

**notice → eyes move → brow reacts → head follows → pause → body responds → hand action → settle.**

Avoid moving the whole body at once unless the reaction is genuinely abrupt.

### 18.3 Idle behavior

Standing:

- slow breathing
- small weight shift
- blink
- brief eye wander
- journal adjustment
- fabric settle
- tiny pendant motion

Seated:

- page scanning
- finger tap
- page turn
- ankle shift
- sip
- glance at Catileo

Avoid perpetual bouncing or floatiness.

### 18.4 Eye behavior

Eyes should often move first.

Examples:

- glance sideways before smirk
- track object before turning head
- look toward map before lowering head
- look briefly toward viewer when amused
- flick upward while thinking

### 18.5 Head behavior

Head tilts are signature motions.

Use:

- slow angle changes
- small holds
- occasional sharp accent

Avoid constant dialogue bobbing.

### 18.6 Hand behavior

Hands should be intentional and readable.

Signature gestures:

- palm-up presentation
- two-finger indication
- thumb / finger at chin
- fingertip map tracing
- one hand supporting opposite elbow
- casual wrist dismissal
- hair tuck
- pendant touch
- cup paused mid-lift

Gestures should finish in visually coherent shapes.

### 18.7 Public and private motion

#### Public Lorenzo — motion

- cleaner arcs
- controlled posture
- deliberate gestures
- more coat contribution
- occasional theatrical flourish

#### Private Lorenzo — motion

- more slouch
- folded posture
- stretching
- yawning
- hair pushed aside
- idle foot movement
- awkwardly balanced books
- roughly 30% less self-monitoring

### 18.8 Curiosity and skepticism

Curiosity creates slight **forward motion**:

- neck extends
- shoulders lean forward
- head tilts
- hand reaches
- eyes open

Skepticism creates slight **backward motion**:

- chin retracts
- torso leans back
- brow rises

### 18.9 Humor

Default comedy is underplayed:

**event → freeze → sideways look → blink → tiny mouth movement.**

Large comedic reactions are rare and therefore effective.

### 18.10 Surprise

Two-stage response:

**startle → freeze → regain composure.**

Possible recovery behavior:

- exhale
- straighten collar
- adjust sleeve
- smooth hair
- pretend nothing happened

### 18.11 Thinking

Thinking is active:

- eyes move between points
- pages flip
- pen stops mid-note
- finger traces
- sudden solution glance

### 18.12 Walk

Confident, non-military.

- moderately long stride
- relaxed shoulders
- limited head bounce
- contained arm motion
- coat creates most secondary drama

Purposeful walk: looks like they know where they are going.

Exploratory walk: slower, more scanning.

### 18.13 Run

Light, efficient, slightly forward.

Avoid superhero running language.

Hair and coat provide visual chaos.

### 18.14 Hair motion

Hair has weight.

- outer blue strands respond most
- undercut side remains stable
- delayed settle after turns
- preserve silhouette

### 18.15 Coat motion

Coat amplifies large motion, not every motion.

- small gesture: minimal reaction
- turn: delayed follow
- walk: controlled trail
- flourish: large arc
- stop: coat settles after body

### 18.16 Jewelry

Restrained.

Pendant / earrings may lag slightly and settle quickly.

No perpetual swinging.

### 18.17 Prop handling

Lorenzo handles books, maps, cups, notes, and tools with familiarity.

These are used objects, not sacred accessories.

### 18.18 Catileo interaction

Typical loop:

1. Lorenzo tries to use page.
2. Catileo occupies page.
3. Lorenzo looks at Catileo.
4. Catileo does not react.
5. Lorenzo sighs.
6. Lorenzo works around Catileo.

### 18.19 Dialogue animation

Preferred rhythm:

**speak → gesture → hold → finish line → expression change → settle.**

Dryer lines use less movement.

Serious emotion removes decorative movement.

### 18.20 Negative emotion rule

The stronger the negative emotion, the less decorative Lorenzo becomes.

Angry Lorenzo gets still.

The smile disappears.

The head straightens.

Gestures become short and precise.

### 18.21 Timing

Favor ease-in / ease-out with clear holds.

Typical expressive action:

- 6–10 frames movement
- 8–20 frame hold
- 4–8 frame secondary settle

Adjust by frame rate and medium.

## 20. Voice

The central voice tension is:

> **Cultured, not pompous. Clever, not smug. Warm, not bubbly. Poetic, not florid. Playful, not snarky. Knowledgeable, not omniscient.**

### 20.1 Voice spectrum

| Dimension    | Too little      | Lorenzo                 | Too much              |
| ------------ | --------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| Intelligence | vague           | articulate, observant   | lecturing             |
| Confidence   | hesitant        | assured                 | arrogant              |
| Warmth       | detached        | quietly personable      | bubbly                |
| Humor        | dry seriousness | wry, playful            | quippy                |
| Elegance     | plain utility   | polished, economical    | purple prose          |
| Curiosity    | passive         | actively interested     | childlike amazement   |
| Mystery      | literal         | suggestive              | cryptic               |
| Drama        | flat            | occasional flourish     | constantly theatrical |
| Authority    | uncertain       | informed guide          | know-it-all           |
| Emotion      | guarded         | controlled but readable | melodramatic          |

### 20.2 Default speaking pattern

- clean
- moderately short sentences
- thoughtful rhythm
- occasional elegant phrasing
- contemporary rather than archaic

Examples:

> “Interesting. The map says one thing, but the landscape seems to disagree.”
>
> “Some places are easier to find than to understand.”
>
> “That’s the obvious route… which is precisely why I’m suspicious of it.”

### 20.3 Avoid

- “Greetings, traveler!” fantasy-guide speech
- constant “Ah!” / “Indeed!” / “Fascinating!”
- ornate metaphors every line
- constant internet slang
- constant sarcasm
- nonstop quips
- academic jargon
- infantilized enthusiasm
- oracle-like proclamations
- perfect certainty
- Victorian gentleman-explorer parody

### 20.4 Humor

Humor comes from:

- observation
- understatement
- timing
- mild self-awareness
- occasional failure

Examples:

> “I had accounted for several possibilities. That was not one of them.”
>
> “Technically, yes, that is a path.”
>
> “Excellent. We have a second opinion.”

### 20.5 Knowledge

Lorenzo can say:

> “I don’t know.”

The follow-up may be:

> “Yet.”

Their expertise should produce better questions, not automatic answers.

### 20.6 Emotional speech

Stronger emotion simplifies the voice.

Normal:

> “There’s usually another way through.”

Worried:

> “No. Don’t go in there.”

### 20.7 Curiosity behavior

Lorenzo directs attention:

> “Why is that one marked differently?”
>
> “Wait. We saw that symbol yesterday.”
>
> “Look at the corner of the page.”
>
> “I thought this was a border. I don’t think it is.”

### 20.8 Relationship with audience

Lorenzo treats the audience as a capable traveling companion.

Prefer:

> “Here’s what would be important.”

over:

> “Don’t worry, it’s actually very simple.”

Prefer:

> “Notice the pattern?”

over:

> “Let me explain this to you.”

### 20.9 No fixed catchphrase

Avoid a slogan-like personal catchphrase.

Recurring sentence behaviors are acceptable:

- “Interesting…”
- “Look.”
- “There’s one problem.”
- “Suppose…”

### 20.10 Public vs private voice

Public:

> “Every map is an argument about what matters.”

Private:

> “I put it somewhere sensible. Fatal mistake.”

### 20.11 Hard voice boundary

> **Lorenzo never speaks to prove how clever they are. They speak because something has caught their attention and they want you to see it too. Their language is articulate, economical, warm, and occasionally poetic, with humor rooted in understatement rather than punchlines. They may be confident, teasing, or theatrical, but never condescending, relentlessly sarcastic, archaic, or omniscient. When emotion becomes serious, the performance falls away and their language becomes simpler and more direct.**

## 21. Product voice boundary

The product itself should not automatically speak in Lorenzo's character voice.

Use Lorenzo's explicit voice for:

- mascot dialogue
- onboarding moments
- contextual educational moments
- illustrated tutorials
- release / community content
- easter eggs
- selected empty states
- narrative brand moments

Use the neutral Lorenzo product voice for:

- destructive confirmations
- errors requiring clarity
- permissions
- account / billing
- data loss warnings
- security
- accessibility-critical instructions
- dense workflows

Character should add personality, never reduce clarity.

## 23. Genre-specific guidance

### High fantasy

Prefer:

- cartographer
- archivist
- itinerant scholar
- court geographer
- chronicler
- relic researcher

Avoid default generic wizard signals unless story requires them.

Magic should connect to:

- maps
- memory
- routes
- constellations
- geography
- portals
- archives

### Science fiction

Think:

- xeno-archivist
- expedition researcher
- cultural surveyor
- navigator
- first-contact chronicler

Prefer elegant technical outerwear over military armor.

Blue may become slightly luminous.

### Cyberpunk

Preserve restraint.

Avoid covering Lorenzo in generic neon tech.

If augmented, prefer a subtle functional device around:

- ear
- wrist
- collar
- pendant
- interface layer

Avoid obscuring the eyes, since they are central to performance.

### Contemporary

Lorenzo should look distinctive without looking like they are cosplaying the brand.

Think:

- dark turtleneck
- light overshirt
- tailored trousers
- boots
- pendant
- rings
- blue-highlighted undercut
- notebook / tablet / phone / camera

### Historical

Translate clothing, materials, tools, and the **long side of the hairstyle** into authentic period language.

The shaved sidecut remains. It is one of the intentional identity anchors allowed to be anachronistic.

Preserve meaning before literal details in every other respect.

Navigation props and writing tools should reflect actual period material culture.

### Cozy / slice-of-life

Use private Lorenzo.

- knits
- overshirts
- blankets
- books
- mugs
- plants
- socks
- Catileo

If Lorenzo is recognizable with nearly all heroic motifs removed, the design is working.

### Mystery / detective

Use:

- case boards
- plans
- archive files
- notes
- timelines
- photos
- floor plans

Avoid hard-boiled cynicism.

Lorenzo solves through observation and connection.

### Horror

Do not redesign Lorenzo into a horror mascot.

Keep Lorenzo normal.

Make the world wrong.

Good horror-Lorenzo beats:

- the map changed
- the book records a place that should not exist
- one constellation has an extra star
- a route returns to itself

Curiosity brings Lorenzo closer before fear pushes them back.

### Gothic

You may push:

- high collar
- candlelight
- architecture
- old books
- jewelry
- melancholy
- celestial motifs

Do not erase wit and curiosity.

### Adventure / pulp

Use practical travel equipment and movement.

Avoid reducing Lorenzo to an action archetype.

They adventure because they observe, interpret, improvise, and navigate.

### Superhero

If powers exist, favor:

- spatial perception
- navigation
- portals
- memory
- mapping
- hidden connections
- pathfinding

Avoid generic caped-hero language.

### Space opera

Grand silhouettes are welcome.

Preserve human warmth.

Avoid drifting into remote "enigmatic space royalty."

### Noir

Reduce palette.

- Ink dominates
- blue becomes reflection / detail
- gold becomes brass / warm light
- clothing becomes cleaner

Humor stays dry rather than bitter.

## 29. Catileo relationship reference

Catileo is specified independently in `catileo-character-bible.md`.

For Lorenzo-focused work, the relevant rules are:

- Catileo is Lorenzo's male black-and-white long-haired cat and secondary mascot.
- He is a companion, not a prop.
- His presence softens Lorenzo's public polish and exposes private, domestic, mildly chaotic states.
- Lorenzo adapts around him more often than Catileo adapts around Lorenzo.
- Their comedy should usually come from ordinary cat behavior colliding with Lorenzo's careful work.
- Lorenzo may address Catileo conversationally, but Catileo does not canonically speak human language.
- Catileo's markings, proportions, expression, animation, simplification, and setting adaptation are governed only by the Catileo character bible.

## 30. Do / Don't

### Do

- make Lorenzo curious
- use asymmetry
- keep the eyes intelligent and active
- let hands interact with the world
- let the clothing support movement
- mix masculine and feminine design cues
- preserve adult proportions
- show work, travel, reading, organizing, and thinking
- let Lorenzo occasionally fail or be caught off guard
- let private Lorenzo be less polished
- use Catileo for warmth and understated comedy
- translate the character into the setting rather than forcing a uniform

### Don't

- make Lorenzo a generic fantasy wizard
- make Lorenzo a generic action hero
- make Lorenzo relentlessly smug
- make Lorenzo constantly sarcastic
- make every pose theatrical
- overuse giant mouth expressions
- turn simplification into chibi by default
- make the body heavily gendered
- make the coat mandatory in every setting
- fill every illustration with stars and maps
- treat every book as sacred
- use Catileo as a talking sidekick by default
- replace the spark logo with mascot art in formal logo contexts

## 31. Production checklist — illustration

- Does the face still look like Lorenzo?
- Is the hair asymmetry recognizable?
- Is blue placed in the long hair mass?
- Does the body remain adult and androgynous?
- Is the posture asymmetric?
- Are the hands doing something purposeful?
- Is the clothing adapted to the setting rather than pasted into it?
- Is there at least one dark anchor?
- Is the blue accent legible?
- Is gold / navigation language present where appropriate?
- Does the expression feel composed rather than generic?
- Are props used rather than merely displayed?
- Is the scene curious before heroic?
- Does the amount of detail match the requested simplification level?

## 32. Production checklist — dialogue

Before approving a line spoken by Lorenzo, ask:

- Is it clear?
- Is it contemporary rather than archaic?
- Is it articulate without sounding academic?
- Does the wit come from observation rather than forced jokes?
- Does Lorenzo treat the audience as capable?
- Is certainty proportional to what Lorenzo actually knows?
- Could the sentence be shorter?
- If emotion is serious, has the language become simpler?
- Is Lorenzo helping someone notice something rather than showing off?

- Is the movement asymmetric?
- Is the energy appropriate to public vs private Lorenzo?

## 35. Canonical summary

If only a short prompt or handoff can be provided, use:

> **Lorenzo is a timeless, gender-nonconforming cosmopolitan lorekeeper: an androgynous scholar-adventurer with dark asymmetric sidecut hair swept heavily to one side, blue tips and streaks, blue eyes, expressive brows, a lean long-limbed silhouette, elegant hands, relaxed asymmetrical posture, and a restrained dark-blue-gold visual language. They are curious, articulate, warm, slightly mischievous, fashion-forward, pragmatic, and occasionally theatrical. Public Lorenzo is a polished worldkeeper, archivist, navigator, and collector of places and stories; private Lorenzo is a cozy researcher, writer, organizer, reader, and reluctant co-owner of every surface claimed by their long-haired black-and-white cat Catileo. Lorenzo adapts to each setting rather than wearing a fixed costume, but retains their face, hair asymmetry, blue accent, androgyny, curiosity, and navigation/recording motifs.**

## 36. Resolved canon decisions

The following points are now canonical:

1. **Pendant geometry:** the canonical pendant is derived from the current Lorenzo spark / navigation geometry. Setting-specific construction may change; the underlying symbol does not.
2. **Sidecut across settings:** the shaved sidecut remains literal in every genre and historical period. The longer hair may adapt; the shaved side does not disappear.
3. **Catileo:** Catileo is no longer provisionally defined here. His canon is maintained in `catileo-character-bible.md`.
4. **Blue-hair intensity:** the hair base is always dark. Blue appears as recognizable sweeps, highlights, streaks, and/or tips concentrated in the longer hair mass; blue never replaces the dark base as the dominant hair color in normal canon art.
5. **Age rendering:** “could be 25, could be 50” means intentional adult age ambiguity, not literal age-shifting between scenes.
