# RFC: Repositories — packaging canonical game systems and world content for reuse across tenants

Status: accepted, with the [amendment](#amendment-decided-with-the-maintainer-2026-09-25) at the end, decided with the maintainer on 2026-09-25. Built as [ADR 0117](../adr/0117-same-tenant-references-by-composite-foreign-keys.md) (same-tenant references), [ADR 0118](../adr/0118-repository-tenants-subscriptions-and-a-gated-read.md) (repository tenants, grants, and the gated read), [ADR 0119](../adr/0119-copying-a-repository-into-a-tenant.md) (the copy), [ADR 0120](../adr/0120-bridge-repositories-and-dependency-manifests.md) (bridges), and [ADR 0121](../adr/0121-repository-updates-and-re-sync.md) (updates and re-sync). Where the amendment and the sections above disagree, the amendment wins.

## Context

[docs/domain/repositories.md](../domain/repositories.md) describes what a repository *is*, in plain language: a reusable setting — a published game system, a homebrew world, or an entire multiverse — that a game can draw on, read live, or copy and diverge from. Nothing has ever designed how. [RFC 0001](0001-core-domain-data-model.md)'s own open question #2 named the core problem directly: prototypes sourced from a shared repository "need to be usable by many tenants without actually living inside any one of them — a plain `entity.tenant_id` can't represent that," and left the likely shape ("repository content lives in its own, separately-authored tables outside the tenant-scoped graph entirely") as a guess, not a design. Open question #3 (multi-game-system stats) separately guessed that the fix might not need a new scoping column at all, and could instead reuse prototype inheritance — a system-agnostic "Sword" with "Sword (D&D 5e)"/"Sword (Blades in the Dark)" variants inheriting from it. [RFC 0018](0018-entity-template.md) (entity_template, proposed, not yet built) independently deferred "cross-tenant, reusable game-system templates" as the same still-open question, and named a not-yet-existing "repository author" role, expected to "eventually own template (and computed-stat) authoring, with a tenant admin subscribing to (copying from) a repository rather than authoring from scratch."

A concrete request sharpened this from an abstract gap into a real proposal: repositories should be able to package **game systems** (mechanical definitions — stat groups, entity ancestries, information types — that a tenant subscribes to and gets copied into its own tenant-specific, editable data), **game content** (a setting's own pre-populated pseudo-entities — NPCs, locations, items, organizations), and **bridge repositories** that connect the two by providing a setting's entities with a specific system's stats (`dnd_faerun`, `pathfinder_faerun`, and `shadowdark_faerun` all bridging the same Faerûn content into three different rule systems). This RFC resolves RFC 0001's open question #2 concretely, confirms and operationalizes its guess at question #3, and names — without solving — the way this makes RFC 0001's still-open question #4 (campaign-relevance of world content, "facts") newly urgent.

This design was produced through a structured, adversarial debate among five independently-researched perspectives (a technical/platform view; a tenant owner/admin view; a GM view; a player view; an author/worldbuilder view) run across three full rounds, followed by two further review passes once the same five perspectives read the resulting document itself rather than just the debate that produced it. One participant (the worldbuilder perspective) initially argued for a materially different core shape — repository content as its own first-class thing, never tenant data at all — and, under cross-examination, reversed that position after stress-testing their own alternative as rigorously as they'd stress-tested the consensus and finding it held up worse; that reversal is folded into the Decision below. The two review passes caught several real gaps between what the debate actually agreed and what the first draft of this document said — most seriously, an incomplete table list in §4 that would have silently blocked core functionality rather than merely narrowing it. Those are fixed below too, and named where it matters that they were gaps, not original design.

## Decision

### 1. A repository is a tenant — specifically, one nobody plays a live game in

`tenant` gains a `kind` column: enum `play` (default) | `repository`. A `kind = repository` tenant never has `Campaign` or `Player` rows — gated at the application layer for this slice (a CHECK/trigger can follow once the shape is proven in practice). This was the most contested single decision in the whole design, and is worth stating plainly why it won over keeping repository content in its own tables outside the tenant graph entirely:

- **Reuse is real, not aspirational.** `entity` and its whole component/extension family — concrete kinds, prototype inheritance (cycle-prevented), stat definitions and values, containment, information/payloads, group membership; the complete list is in §4 — plus every view built over them (`v_effective_stat`, ADR 0037/0039), work unmodified. A parallel, non-tenant-scoped schema would need every one of these — the cycle-prevention trigger, the recursive stat resolution, the class-table-inheritance payload shape — taught to also traverse a second graph, forever, for every future feature that touches entities.
- **The alternative's own strongest form was tried and found worse.** A single `entity` table with a `repository_id` column as an alternative to `tenant_id` (one shared graph, not a parallel schema) was proposed and stress-tested directly. It fails on a different, more dangerous axis than the one it was meant to avoid: every existing query, view, and RLS policy that currently treats `tenant_id` as *the* scoping column would need to learn a second, mutually-exclusive case — most consequentially `v_effective_stat`'s own recursive prototype resolution, the most complex and performance-sensitive query in the domain model. A missed branch there is a silent, unenumerable failure (an inherited stat quietly resolves wrong) rather than a loud, obvious one (a repository shows up somewhere in an admin screen it shouldn't). The tenant-flavored approach is additive purely at the **policy** layer — invisible to every query and view that already exists — rather than at the **schema** layer, visible to all of them.

### 2. Authorship is Membership on the repository-tenant, for now — explicitly not the final answer

Whoever holds `Membership(role=OWNER)` on a `kind=repository` tenant can edit its content. This is RFC 0018's still-unbuilt "repository author" role, and it costs nothing new to stand up: invitations, notifications, and the tenant admin surfaces already work. It is **not** claimed to be free, and it is **not** the final design — see [Not in Scope](#not-in-scope).

**Hard invariant, enforced at the data layer, not just by convention**: `tenant.kind` is immutable after creation (or settable only while the tenant holds zero content — the exact mechanism is an implementation choice, not a design one). This is required specifically so an existing, populated play-tenant can never be "promoted" into a repository after the fact — publishing always means creating a fresh, distinct tenant. Without this, someone could route around every other guardrail in this document by simply relabeling a live tenant. This is what makes the concrete case that motivated re-opening this question — a solo worldbuilder who also runs unrelated solo campaigns needing to share exactly one setting with co-authors, without exposing those unrelated campaigns — actually hold: the shared setting lives in its own, purpose-created repository-tenant from the start; nobody is ever a member of the worldbuilder's personal tenant, and tenant-to-tenant RLS isolation between the two is exactly as strong as between any two unrelated tenants today, because nothing about this design weakens it.

**Who can create a `kind=repository` tenant in the first place is a separate question from who can create an ordinary one, and this RFC takes an explicit position rather than leaving it to default.** For this slice, publishing reuses the same gate as ordinary tenant creation ([ADR 0033](../adr/0033-tenant-creation-and-update-api.md)'s `tenant-creator` Authgear role). A distinct, stricter publishing-specific role is a reasonable future step — trusting someone to run their own tenant is a smaller grant than trusting them to publish something other tenants will build on top of — and is named in [Not in Scope](#not-in-scope) rather than silently assumed away.

### 3. `repository_subscription` — the only new access-control table, a persistent grant, not a mode switch

`repository_subscription(repository_tenant_id, subscriber_tenant_id, created_at, created_by)`. A DB-enforced trigger CHECK requires `repository_tenant_id` to reference a `tenant.kind = 'repository'` row. This is the single mechanism that makes the RLS extension below safe by construction rather than by discipline: the exception is **structurally incapable** of granting one ordinary tenant read access into another ordinary tenant, independent of whether every future line of application code gets it right.

The row is a plain grant, not a state a subscription is locked into. Read access via §4's policy exists for as long as the row does. Browsing a repository's current live content and triggering a copy (§5) are both just things a grant-holder can do at any time, not two mutually exclusive modes — triggering a copy again later, against the same still-live grant, is exactly what re-sync (§7) is. (An earlier draft of this RFC put a `mode: live | frozen` column directly on this table; dropped, since nothing in §4's policy actually gated on it, and it would have made re-sync's own ongoing staleness checks — which need the grant to keep working after a copy has already happened — inconsistent with a subscription that had supposedly "become" frozen.)

**Subscribing to a repository that itself depends on others is one confirmed action, not several.** A bridge repository's own subscriptions (§5) are its declared dependencies. Subscribing a tenant to a bridge surfaces the full transitive dependency set — the bridge plus everything it in turn depends on — as a single manifest, confirmed once. Never a silent cascade of extra grants the subscriber didn't see, and never a requirement to separately discover and subscribe to each dependency by hand; picking "D&D 5e + Forgotten Realms" should be one action, not three.

### 4. A second, narrow, read-only RLS policy on every table repository content can populate

An earlier draft of this section named six tables by picking one representative per concern, missing the pattern entirely. A further review pass then caught that the fix itself was still incomplete: `stat_group`/`stat_definition` and `payload` were absent from even the corrected list, and the stated exclusion of `knowledge`/`ownership` turned out to be factually wrong once checked directly against those models' own documentation. All three are corrected below, and — since this is now the second and third time an enumeration alone has let something slip through — stated as a principle first, not just a list.

**The governing principle**: any table that can hold data belonging to a repository's own canonical content — however deeply nested in class-table inheritance, and whether or not it references an entity directly — needs this same policy. Any table that can only ever hold data relative to a real player or a real campaign (subset-visible content, player-authored content, campaign linkage, tenant administration) never does, because a repository-tenant never has the players or campaigns that content would be relative to (§1).

Applying that principle to the schema as it stands today, the complete set: `entity` itself; its concrete-kind extensions (`item`, `item_instance`, `being`, `character`, and any future concrete kind added the same way); every table that describes or attaches mechanical, relational, or narrative content — `entity_prototype`, `stat_group`, `stat_definition`, `entity_stat_group`, `entity_stat`, `containment`, `information`, `payload`, `group_member`, `knowledge`, `ownership`; and the concrete extensions of those (`payload`'s four kinds — `payload_description`, `payload_number`, `payload_picture`, `payload_document`). Each gains a second, additive, `FOR SELECT`-only permissive policy alongside its existing `tenant_id = your own` policy, of the same shape:

```sql
CREATE POLICY repository_read ON entity FOR SELECT
  USING (tenant_id IN (
    SELECT repository_tenant_id FROM repository_subscription
    WHERE subscriber_tenant_id = current_setting('app.tenant_id')::uuid
  ));
```

**On `knowledge` and `ownership` specifically**: an earlier draft excluded both on the reasoning that repository content has no real knowers or owners. Checked directly against the code and found wrong. `Ownership`'s own model documentation notes it "isn't restricted to characters as an owner at the schema level" — a faction or a place could plausibly own something. `Knowledge.knower_entity_id` can name a character *or group* entity with zero `Player` involvement (`knower_player_id` is a separate, independently-nullable column) — a repository can legitimately pre-author "Elminster already knows secret X" as canonical lore, entity-knower only, which is the only form possible on a repository-tenant anyway, since it never has `Player` rows. Both tables are included on the same basis as everything else here.

**On `group_member` specifically**: it does two different jobs in this codebase — letting a group act as a *knower* (gating who sees what), and recording *who belongs to a group* as a plain fact (a guild's or faction's roster). Repository content has genuine use for the second — a content repository pre-authoring "these NPCs are members of this organization" is exactly the kind of world content this RFC exists to make copyable — and, per the correction above, some genuine use for the first too, in its entity-only form. A group copied in from a repository is a roster until a tenant deliberately decides otherwise; it does not automatically become eligible as a live visibility cohort just by having been copied, since §10 already limits repository-sourced knowledge to the two knower-independent tiers regardless of what `group_member` rows exist.

**Deliberately excluded, and why**: anything campaign/membership/notification-shaped (`campaign`, `player`, `membership`, `character_player`, `campaign_gm`, `notification`, `audit_log`, ...) — a repository-tenant never holds rows in any of these by construction (§1), which is precisely what the governing principle above predicts, not a separate carve-out.

**Worth naming for whoever implements the copy walk (§5/§6), though it changes nothing about which tables need the policy above**: `entity`/`entity_prototype` are the pair where a *bridge* repository's own content genuinely spans more than one source repository — a bridge's `entity_prototype` edge has one parent in a content-repository's local copy and one in a system-repository's local copy (§9), which needs the cross-repository id-re-targeting logic §5's worked example describes. Every other table in this list only ever references entities within its own single source repository; copying it is a plain re-parent with no cross-repository lookup involved.

Postgres ORs multiple permissive policies together automatically. The base policy's own `WITH CHECK` is untouched — writes can never target a subscribed-to repository, even under a buggy `USING` clause, because the write path never changes at all. Two further requirements, named as **required scope for this RFC's implementation, not optional hardening**:

- The exception policy is `FOR SELECT` only, never `FOR ALL`. Its worst-case failure mode is leaking already-published read access, never a write.
- This codebase already has a standing, per-table convention of directly testing RLS isolation (`test_entity.py`, `test_entity_prototype.py`, `test_containment.py`, `test_information.py`, `test_knowledge.py` all assert tenant-isolation behavior against `current_setting('app.tenant_id')` today). Extend it with two new assertions per affected table: *no subscription row exists → zero cross-tenant visibility*, and *a subscription row exists → SELECT-only visibility, writes still rejected*. A future refactor that breaks isolation breaks this suite mechanically, every time — this is what "safe by construction" means in a codebase with real CI gates, as distinct from a policy whose safety depends on being carefully re-read during every future change nearby.

Whether this two-policy shape is a smaller, safer version of a "cross-tenant read" than a live gameplay-resolution read across tenants would be — rather than the same risk in a thinner disguise — was challenged directly during this design's review and is worth answering plainly, since it is the same underlying primitive the earlier "cross-tenant repositories" question deliberately fenced off pending its own RFC: **yes, it is the same primitive**, and this is that RFC. What makes it a different proposal from what was fenced is that it is narrowly scoped (SELECT-only), grant-gated (structurally, not just by policy), exercised once per explicit subscribe/re-sync action rather than continuously on every gameplay read, and leaves a permanent audit trail (§7) — a live-gameplay-resolution read across tenants would fail on all four of those properties at once, exercising the same boundary on every stat lookup forever, with a policy bug exploitable continuously and silently rather than at one loggable moment.

### 5. Copy powers gameplay; live mode is for browsing and diffing only

Reading a repository-tenant's current content live (§3, §4's policy) exists for two purposes only: browsing a repository before deciding to subscribe to it, and diffing an already-copied subscription against its current source (§7). **It is never used for runtime stat or entity resolution in an actual campaign.** Triggering a copy walks the repository-tenant's reachable subgraph (its entities, `entity_prototype` edges, `stat_group`/`stat_definition`, `entity_stat`/`entity_stat_group`, `containment`, `information`/`payload`, `group_member`, `knowledge`, `ownership`), mints fresh ids in the subscriber's own tenant, and re-parents every `tenant_id`. Once complete, the subscriber's data is ordinary, self-contained tenant data — `v_effective_stat` resolves it with zero cross-tenant joins, indistinguishable from anything authored by hand.

Copying must happen in **dependency order**, and this has a real consequence for bridge repositories specifically: a bridge repository (e.g. `dnd_faerun`) is itself a subscriber of the repositories it bridges (`Faerûn`, `D&D 5e`) — it holds its **own local copies** of both before its own authors create any cross-referencing `entity_prototype` edge. This means no `entity_prototype` row is ever cross-tenant at rest, at any layer, including during bridge-repository authoring itself — which means bridge-repository authoring is never "live" against its own upstream dependencies. This is a deliberate choice, not an accepted gap: deciding how a setting's content and a system's mechanics fit together is curatorial, editorial work that should be reviewed and re-published deliberately when either dependency changes, not a live pass-through of whichever upstream edit landed most recently — the same reasoning, and the same answer, as why an end tenant doesn't get automatic upstream updates either (§7).

**Worked example** — a tenant subscribing to Faerûn under three possible systems: repositories `Faerûn` (content), `D&D 5e`/`Pathfinder`/`Shadowdark` (systems), `dnd_faerun` (a bridge, itself already holding local copies of `Faerûn` and `D&D 5e`, with its own authors having created e.g. an entity "Blackstaff (D&D 5e)" inheriting from both local copies, plus its own `EntityStat` overrides). A tenant subscribing to `dnd_faerun` is shown its declared dependencies (`Faerûn`, `D&D 5e`) as one manifest (§3) and confirms all three together. The copy then proceeds in dependency order: first `D&D 5e` (system prototypes and stat groups land with new ids), then `Faerûn` (Waterdeep, Elminster, Blackstaff land with new ids, system-agnostic), then `dnd_faerun` (its cross-repository edges and overrides get re-created using the subscriber's own now-local copies of both endpoints, resolved via the provenance table in §6). The tenant now holds "its own Blackstaff IS-A its own Staff(D&D5e)," entirely within its own tenant.

### 6. Deep clone by default, local additions always layered — never a fork

Freezing clones the **whole reachable prototype chain**, not just an entity's own local data with a live pointer back to shared definitions. A tenant's subsequent additions (a house-rule "Honor" stat) are ordinary new rows — a plain `INSERT` into the tenant's own `stat_definition`, or a new local prototype inheriting from the frozen copy — sitting beside the copied content, never editing it, never requiring anything a user would recognize as "forking." This resolves two requirements at once that initially looked like they pointed in different directions: it is what makes adding a house rule feel like an ordinary, everyday action rather than a special operation, and it is what makes a subscriber's own data durable against the source repository disappearing later (an author's account is deleted, a repository is unpublished, a table's connection is down) — once copied, a subscriber's data has zero runtime dependency on the source continuing to exist, for as long as the subscription has ever been copied even once.

Per-source-table provenance makes "never editing the copy" verifiable rather than assumed:

- `repository_copy_link_entity(subscriber_entity_id, source_entity_id, source_repository_tenant_id, source_updated_at, copied_at)`
- `repository_copy_link_stat_group(subscriber_stat_group_id, source_stat_group_id, source_repository_tenant_id, source_updated_at, copied_at)`
- `repository_copy_link_stat_definition(subscriber_stat_definition_id, source_stat_definition_id, source_repository_tenant_id, source_updated_at, copied_at)`

(Real foreign keys into each real table — matching this schema's own existing preference for class-table precision over a polymorphic `(source_table, source_id)` pair with no enforceable FK, the same idiom `payload`'s own concrete tables already use.) A locally-authored row like Honor simply has no corresponding row in any of these tables — indistinguishable from copied content in every other respect, safe from re-sync by construction, not by convention. Whether `information`/`payload`/`group_member`/`containment` need the same per-row provenance is an open v1-vs-v1.1 sizing question, not resolved here — but until whichever of them gets it, that content is copied once at subscribe time and is not offered for re-sync at all, since re-sync (§7) has no row to check staleness against without a provenance link.

**Unsubscribing does not touch what was already copied.** Deleting a `repository_subscription` row revokes the live-read grant (§3, §4) and stops future re-sync availability — it does not retroactively affect any rows already cloned into the subscriber's tenant, which are that tenant's own ordinary data by this point (per §1's whole premise) with no live dependency on the grant that originally produced them. Any now-orphaned `repository_copy_link_*` rows simply stop having anything to compare staleness against; they are not deleted, so the subscriber's own audit trail of where their content originally came from survives even after unsubscribing.

### 7. Re-sync is explicit, row-scoped, and never silent

Comparing a `repository_copy_link_*` row's recorded `source_updated_at` against the source row's current `updated_at` gives a cheap per-row staleness check. This must be **surfaced proactively** — through whatever the product's own notification/dashboard mechanism looks like by the time this ships, not narrowly frozen to today's web-inbox-only shape — rather than left for a GM to remember to go looking for; a perfectly safe mechanism nobody is ever told to use might as well not exist. Applying a specific update is always a deliberate, reviewable action against one specific row (a real diff of what changed, never a blanket "re-sync everything"), which per-row provenance makes a normal, scoped operation rather than a special case. Content with no provenance link yet (§6) is simply not offered for re-sync at all — not a special case to design around, a direct consequence of there being nothing to compare it against.

**Collisions are named as a real case, never resolved silently.** If an upstream repository later adds content that collides with a tenant's own existing addition (an upstream "Honor" stat landing on top of a tenant's own pre-existing "Honor"), the collision must be surfaced to the person applying the update as an explicit choice (rename / merge / skip) at the moment it's detected — never picked automatically, even by a definition of "safe" that seems obviously correct. A silent-but-sensible default is still a silent change to someone's game.

**Additions vs. corrections** are two different shapes of update worth naming distinctly, though both use the same mechanism: pulling a correction to a stat a tenant already has a copy of, versus pulling a stat that's new to a group the tenant already copied (no existing local row to update, a new one to add). The re-sync UX needs to handle both, not just the correction case.

### 8. Availability is three distinct things, not a boolean

- **Draft vs. published.** A repository-tenant has no content-relevant audience structure until explicitly published — content still being drafted with co-authors must never be visible to any subscriber or listing before that point.
- **Unlisted vs. discoverable.** This RFC scopes v1 to **unlisted-only**: a repository is subscribable only by tenants its owner explicitly grants access to. Public discoverability (a browsable marketplace) is explicitly out of scope (§Not in Scope) — it implies moderation, abuse-reporting, and trust signals nobody has designed, and naming that honestly now is better than backing into it unscoped.
- **The grant is a first-class record** — `repository_subscription` itself — not a boolean. It is created manually by the repository owner today, but structured so that *how* a grant gets created (manual approval, an invite link, a future payment step) is swappable later without redesigning the subscribe/copy mechanism underneath it.

### 9. Multiple prototype inheritance is the bridge mechanism

This operationalizes RFC 0001's own open question #3 rather than inventing something new: a bridge entity like "Blackstaff (D&D 5e)" is one ordinary entity with two prototype parents — a content-repository's entity (identity, lore, portrait) and a system-repository's entity (mechanical stat groups) — exactly RFC 0001's "system-agnostic prototype, system-specific prototypes inherit from it" idea, made concrete by having the bridge repository hold its own local copies of both (§5).

### 10. Knowledge on repository content is necessarily narrower than on live campaign content — but only while it's still repository content

Only the two campaign-independent visibility tiers — GM-only and public — are meaningful on a repository-tenant's *own* pre-copy content. Subset-visible and player-authored both presuppose real characters and real players, neither of which exist until a copy meets an actual campaign. This is not a gap in the knowledge system; it is the boundary of what a repository is for. It is not, however, a permanent ceiling on the copy itself: once content has been cloned into a subscriber's own tenant (§5/§6), it is ordinary tenant data like anything else, and a GM can add subset-visible or player-authored text to it exactly as they would to anything they authored by hand.

### 11. Cross-system stat coexistence is free at storage, real work — and a real gate — at the presentation layer

Per [ADR 0079](../adr/0079-account-hub-roster-reuse-and-being-handoff.md), roster reuse is same-tenant, cross-campaign — the *same* entity linked into a second campaign's roster. Multiple `entity_prototype` inheritance and multiple `entity_stat_group` attachment already let that one entity hold stat groups from two different subscribed systems simultaneously, with zero new schema; `v_effective_stat` resolves both without modification. What does **not** exist today: any way to filter a read down to "only the stats relevant to campaign X" — `campaign.game_system` (verified: a bare free-text column) has no structural link to any `stat_group`. Making that real is scoped, sequenced follow-up work (§Not in Scope), not a blocking prerequisite for the rest of this RFC, since the storage half already works unmodified.

**Until that filtered view ships, the capability to roster the same character into a second campaign running a different system than its first must not be exposed — enforced, not just documented as a rule.** Concretely: the existing roster-link write path (`PUT .../characters/{id}/players/{id}`, ADR 0079) gains an interim guard comparing the two campaigns' `game_system` values and rejecting the link — fail closed on an unset or mismatched value, not fail open — until the real structural link and filtered view (§Not in Scope) exist to make the result legible instead of undifferentiated. Same-system reuse, which already works today, is entirely unaffected by this restriction.

## Not in scope

- **A finer repository-author role.** Plain tenant `Membership` (§2) is what this RFC builds. A repository with multiple active co-authors may eventually want to distinguish a proposer from a publisher/maintainer — RFC 0018's still-unbuilt "repository author" role — but that need has not yet been demonstrated in practice and is deliberately left for its own follow-up rather than designed speculatively here.
- **A publishing-specific gate distinct from ordinary tenant creation** (§2). Reuses `tenant-creator` for now; a stricter, separate role for "can publish a repository other tenants build on" is a reasonable later step, not designed here.
- **The "facts" mechanism** (RFC 0001 open question #4). Copying a whole content-repository into a tenant makes its entities *available*, not automatically relevant to any one campaign's unfolding story — an NPC nobody's met is not meaningfully different, post-copy, from an NPC nobody's authored. Unlike the other items in this section, this is not a "we chose not to" deferral: this RFC's own Decision 5 is what turns this from a hypothetical into a real, forced problem the first time anyone actually copies a repository into a live tenant. It needs an answer on the same timeline as the first real implementation of copy-and-freeze, not an arbitrary future one — named here so that urgency isn't lost by the time this reaches an ADR.
- **Public discoverability / a repository marketplace** (§8). Unlisted-and-granted only for v1.
- **Monetization or licensing of published content.** The subscription-grant record (§8) is deliberately structured so a payment step could plug into "how a grant gets created" later; no payment mechanism is designed here.
- **Migrating `campaign.game_system` to a real reference, and the filtered per-campaign stat view** (§11). Real, scoped follow-up, sequenced after this RFC's core mechanism ships — required before the interim guard in §11 can be lifted, even though it does not block anything else in this document.
- **Per-row provenance for `information`/`payload`/`group_member`/`containment`** (§6) — an open v1-vs-v1.1 sizing decision, not a design question, deliberately left for whoever picks up implementation to size against real re-sync granularity needs. Until it lands, that content is copied once and is simply not offered for re-sync (§7).
- **The isolation-test-suite extension and the DB-enforced `tenant.kind`/subscription-target checks (§2, §4) are required implementation scope, not optional hardening** — named here explicitly so they cannot be quietly dropped as "nice to have" once this becomes an ADR.

## Consequences

- Every future surface that lists, filters, or offers tenants for selection (a tenant switcher, campaign-creation flows, anything resembling "pick a tenant") must be aware of `tenant.kind` and correctly exclude repository-tenants, or they will leak into UI that assumes every tenant is a play tenant. This is real, ongoing surface area — not eliminated by this design, only concentrated at the product-UI layer rather than the query layer, which was the explicit trade this RFC makes.
- A repository-tenant's rows are still, mechanically, ordinary tenant-scoped data with a real `tenant_id` — just belonging to a tenant nobody plays a live game in. Every future feature built against `entity`/`stat_group`/etc. continues to work against repository-tenants unmodified; the cost of this design shows up in product surfaces (the point above), not in the domain model itself.
- Deep-clone-by-default means subscribing to a large repository is a real, potentially slow, bulk-copy operation, not an instant reference — performance of the copy walk at real repository sizes is unaddressed here and will need attention once real repositories exist to measure against.
- The activity log ([ADR 0063](../adr/0063-tenant-activity-log.md)/[ADR 0084](../adr/0084-activity-log-coverage-and-member-removal-notice.md)) should cover repository-tenant mutations through its existing mechanism — no second audit system is proposed or needed.
- A tenant owner needs one screen listing every repository their tenant is subscribed to and what each one contributed — the same "one place tells me what's actually true about my tenant" shape this project's own admin surfaces already converge on elsewhere. Not designed here; named so it doesn't get lost the way it nearly did between the debate that agreed on it and this document.
- This RFC deliberately reopens the same underlying primitive ("an authenticated read across a tenant RLS boundary") that an earlier, unscoped version of "cross-tenant repositories" was fenced off from building. The fence was against building that primitive undesigned; this document is the design the fence was waiting for — narrowly scoped, grant-gated at the data layer, SELECT-only, and audited — not a way around it.

## Amendment: decided with the maintainer, 2026-09-25

*Added when this RFC was accepted. The design was checked against the code as it stood on `main` that day. Several ADRs had landed in the two days since the debate, and two of the RFC's claims no longer held. Each point below names what changed and why. Where it disagrees with a section above, this wins.*

### A1. The read policy is gated, never always on (replaces part of §4)

§4 says the second policy is "additive purely at the policy layer — invisible to every query and view that already exists". That isn't true of this codebase. `v_effective_stat` starts from an unfiltered `FROM entity e` and relies on RLS alone to scope it; so does the prototype-cycle trigger. An always-on policy would make every stat read in a subscriber tenant also walk every subscribed repository's prototype graph, and any query that leans on RLS alone would start seeing repository rows.

So the policy only applies inside a request that has asked for it. The code that browses, plans, copies, or diffs a repository sets a second, transaction-local setting, `app.repository_tenant_id`. The policy lets a row through only when its `tenant_id` is that repository, the current tenant holds a grant for it, and the repository is published. An ordinary request never sets it, so §5's "live reads never power gameplay" is enforced by the database, not by discipline. Everything else §4 asks for stays: `FOR SELECT` only, grant-gated, and the per-table isolation tests. See ADR 0118.

### A2. References stay inside one tenant by construction (new prerequisite)

Every foreign key between two tenant tables is single-column today (`entity_prototype.prototype_id → entity.id`, and so on). Only application code keeps both ends in one tenant, and a foreign-key check bypasses RLS. Once repository rows are readable, a single existence check that relies on RLS alone could let a subscriber store an edge into a repository — exactly the cross-tenant row at rest that §5 promises can't exist. Every such foreign key becomes composite, `(…, tenant_id)`, before any cross-tenant read exists. See ADR 0117.

### A3. The table list, brought up to date (extends §4)

Tables that landed after the debate and hold repository content: `computed_stat`, `computed_stat_linear`, `computed_stat_comparison` (ADR 0104), `stat_definition_enum_value` (ADR 0103), `entity_slug` (ADR 0107), and `content_reference` (ADR 0110). `content_reference` is derived from description text, so it is recomputed after a copy rather than copied. `information_type` (ADR 0101) is a global catalog with no `tenant_id` and needs nothing. `entity_change` (ADR 0099) and `campaign_invite` (ADR 0092) are player- or campaign-relative and are excluded by §4's own principle. `item.in_public_catalog` (ADR 0116) is copied as the author set it. The provenance and copy-record tables this design adds join the list too, because a bridge's subscribers need to read them (A8).

### A4. Collisions start at the first copy, not at re-sync (extends §7)

`stat_group` and `stat_definition` names are unique per tenant, and so are slugs. A tenant that already has a "Strength" collides the moment it copies a system repository. §7's explicit rename / merge / skip choice therefore belongs in the first copy, and a copy with an unresolved collision is refused with the list of choices it needs. See ADR 0119.

### A5. Published is a real state (gives §8 a place in the schema)

`tenant.published_at`, null until the repository's owner publishes it. The read policy requires it. Publishing again later is how an owner announces an update (A7).

### A6. The cross-system roster guard is deferred (replaces §11's interim guard)

`campaign.game_system` is required free text. A guard comparing it would reject "D&D 5e" against "dnd5e" today, before any repository exists, and would change ADR 0079's behaviour for every existing user. The guard moves to the same follow-up as making `game_system` a real reference, which §11 already names. Until then cross-system reuse behaves exactly as it does now.

### A7. Updates are found by comparing snapshots, not timestamps (replaces §6's `source_updated_at`)

§6 and §7 compare a copy link's recorded `source_updated_at` with the source row's current `updated_at`. That misses most real changes. `entity.updated_at` only moves when the `entity` row itself is written. A changed stat value moves `entity_stat.updated_at`, and an added or removed prototype edge moves nothing at all, since `entity_prototype` has no timestamps. So each copy link records a snapshot of what was copied instead: the row's own fields plus, for an entity, its prototypes, stat groups, stat values, formulas, and slug, with every id translated to its origin. Re-sync compares three versions: the snapshot, the source now, and the local copy now. That gives the real diff §7 asks for and tells an upstream correction from a local edit. See ADR 0121.

§6's own scoping is kept: `information`, `payload`, `containment`, `ownership`, `group_member`, and `knowledge` are copied once and are not offered for re-sync in this version.

### A8. Bridges carry only what they authored, and every dependency needs its own grant (makes §3, §5, and §9 concrete)

- **What a bridge carries.** A repository's "own" rows are the ones that aren't copies. A subscriber copies only a repository's own rows. Where an own row points at a copied one (Blackstaff (D&D 5e)'s prototype edge to the bridge's copy of Staff), the reference is re-targeted onto the subscriber's own copy of the same origin row. A bridge's edits to its copies of its dependencies don't travel downstream. To change how an upstream entity behaves under a system, the bridge authors a new entity inheriting from it, which is §9's mechanism anyway.
- **Every copy link points at an origin.** Because only own rows are ever copied, every copy link names the row's origin, never a copy of a copy. Re-targeting is one lookup, however deep the chain.
- **Grants aren't transitive.** A bridge's owner can't grant access to a repository somebody else owns. The dependency manifest lists every repository a copy needs and whether the subscriber holds a grant for each. A copy with a missing grant is refused, naming the repository. What stays "one confirmed action" is the copy: one call copies everything the manifest still needs, in dependency order, and reuses dependencies the subscriber already copied.

See ADR 0120.

### A9. Copied world content and campaign GMs (maintainer's decision on the "facts" question)

Accepted for this version as a known gap, not a blocker: until a campaign's characters reach copied content, a campaign GM doesn't see its GM-only text. Only the tenant's OWNER and ORGA do (ADR 0035/0046/0096). The "facts" mechanism stays open (RFC 0001 question 4).

### A10. Smaller decisions

- **`tenant.kind` can never change.** A trigger rejects any update to it, not "settable while empty", so the check is trivially sound.
- **Campaigns can't exist in a repository.** Checked by a trigger on `campaign`, not just in the application, since an immutable `kind` makes that sound from the start. Players, GM grants, and invites all hang off a campaign, so they're impossible too.
- **Grants are a repository-side act.** The repository's OWNER grants a tenant access by its id, and either side can remove the grant. The subscribing tenant's members are notified. What a copy produced is recorded on the subscriber's side, in its own table, so it survives the grant being removed (§6).
- **This implementation is API only.** Screens for repositories in `apps/account-hub` and `apps/inventory-web` are a later, separately scoped piece of work. Authoring a repository's content needs nothing new: a repository is a tenant, so its members use the same catalog, stat, and information screens as anywhere else.
