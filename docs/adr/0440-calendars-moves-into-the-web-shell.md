# 0440 - Calendars moves into the web shell

Status: accepted, decided with the maintainer on 2026-11-08. Part of [RFC 0050](../rfcs/0050-the-web-interface.md); it implements the Calendars part of UI-E ([#1016](https://github.com/ramsesoriginal/lorenzo/issues/1016)) with [ADR 0420](0420-calendars-and-sessions-redesign.md) and the design in [docs/design/web/calendars-sessions](../design/web/calendars-sessions/README.md), on the shell of [ADR 0426](0426-the-web-shell-grows-in-app-shell-opt-in.md), as the hub did in [ADR 0428](0428-the-account-hub-moves-into-the-web-shell.md).

## Context

Calendars draws its own page as a one-row header, a `wrap` and a footer, and moves between its pages with Astro's client router, so what it holds in memory (the read cache, prefetched Events) lives from one page to the next. The design (CS1 to CS7) puts the month, Events and Clocks in the web shell's frame, with the month as a grid beside a day panel.

## Decision

- **The frame is the web shell** (`AppPage shell="web"`, the `list-detail` template). The tabs are Calendar, Events and Clocks. Calendar stays the current tab on a calendar's page and on an entry (`NavItem.also`). Clocks is offered to a library that has a clock and to anyone who can write, since the first clock is made there.
- **The shell can be bound again.** An app with a client router binds the shell on every page. `bindShell` now lets go of what the previous page held on the document and the window (the keys, the docked sheet's Escape and outside press, the unsent warning, the clock) before it binds, so nothing answers for a header that is gone.
- **The account button's name holds the letters it shows** ("DO, account menu"), which is what a screen reader and WCAG 2.5.3 want, and which the shell's accessibility check found.
- **A page that says its own loading and a page that has a welcome both work;** Calendars keeps its welcome, which is what a signed-out visitor sees.
- **The library switcher, the palette, the apps sheet and the status bar** are the shell's. The status bar says "Synced" with the time; the in-world date is for a Session (CS1), so Calendars shows none.
- **Appearance is the shell's dialog.** The old light/dark toggle is gone; a signed-in person chooses in Appearance (the account menu), and a signed-out visitor has the system's.
- **Home is the list of calendars, drawn as the design draws a Home (D16):** "Calendars" with the library's name, a primary "New calendar" that opens the form that makes one (for whoever can write; nobody else is offered it), a card for each calendar (its name is the link, the whole card opens it, and it says when the calendar last changed), then the blocks every Home shares. **Continue** is the calendars and entries last opened in this library, kept in this browser (`lorenzo:calendars:recent:<library>`, named in the privacy policy), and **What do you want to do?** is every deployed app, from the API's registry. The logic of Continue (`rememberPage`, `parseRecent`, `agoText`) is in `@lorenzo/app-shell`, and the hub uses it from there. The links to Events and Clocks that Home had are the tabs.
- **The month's head, strip and grid as drawn (CS2, CS6):** the page's heading is still the calendar's name (small, with the year after it), and the segment on show is the large title under it; on the right are Month and Whole year as one control, the year with its buttons, the calendar's own page and, for whoever can write, "Edit the calendar", which opens the definition editor (it is not shown until then). The select of segments is a strip: a chip for each, with its days and how much is written as five dots, a festival with a double edge, and a leap segment the year does not have kept in its place and not openable; it is one row that scrolls, and Page Up and Page Down change segment. The grid has hairlines, a muted row of weekdays, the selected day framed, and each day shows its moons and up to two marks with "+N" (a note, an Event, a Fact, a recurring observance: each its own symbol, the Events told from the Facts by the library's one list of Events) in place of the old snippets of text; the words of each mark stay on the page for a screen reader and are in the day panel in full, which docks beside the grid from 1100px and goes under it below.
- **The day panel as drawn (CS5, CS6):** over the date's name it says which day of the week and of the year it is ("Donnerstag · day 126 of Year 3286"), and under it is **Add to this day**, a menu: a note on this day, an Event, a Fact, an existing Event or Fact, and (for a recurring observance) the rule, which are the forms the panel had as folding bars, now opened from the menu and not there until asked. Opening a date creates nothing; writing a note, or any of these, is what makes its period.
- **The whole year as drawn (CS2):** a row for each segment, with its name, its size and a quiet Notes, and a square for each day, darker for more written on it (one to three, from what the day's summary holds), and a line that says so. The squares are the same days with the same reads and the same four-date limit as the month's; they carry no words, so a day's name is its button's, and its moons are the button's description. A day chosen opens in the panel, as in the month.
- **Events as drawn, with what the API gives (CS3):** "Events" with a primary **New Event** that opens the form that makes one (the bar that did is not there until then); the search and the list order in a card, the participant and causal filters under it, and the list as one frame with a hairline between rows, an Event's name as its link, and a chip for what its place in the story order rests on: "recorded" (causal or recorded order) or "linearized" (a rule placed it, which is display order and not a claim), with its place in the order when the list is in story order. The words the chip stands for are on the page for a screen reader. Under each name is where it is dated and who took part ("Undated · With Aster"), read for each row through the entry's own dates and participants (the list does not carry them), and the **time filter** (CS2, CS7) over them: a strip of one calendar's year (the calendar and the year are chosen beside it), a segment bar with a tick on each day something is dated on, taller where there is more, and a range with two handles that move by pointer or by Arrow, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End. A range keeps the Events dated inside it, in that calendar and year, and "Any time" clears it. It is the shared component CS7 asks for, `@lorenzo/time-filter`, drawn with the brand's `.time-filter`; Maps' date lens is its next user. The list does not say a date, so the filter reads every Event's dates in the browser; a period filter on the list itself is [#1135](https://github.com/ramsesoriginal/lorenzo/issues/1135).
- **An entry's page, as cards (CS3, screen 08):** the page's head is the entry's name over its kinds; the panels that were stacked (clock readings, reading on another clock, Order and causes, participants, learning, visibility, dates, notes, name and audience) are cards in two columns from 1100px and one below it, each with a heading. The panels themselves, and what each reads and writes, are unchanged; the cards that fold still fold.
- **Clocks and the definition editor (CS5, screens 11 and 13):** the list of clocks has the same card as the list of calendars (its name is the link and the whole card opens it), and the editor of a definition has a segment on one row, its name, days and kind with what moves or removes it beside them, its week and leap rule folded under it, and the columns named once, over the first row.
- **The links to the rest of the system (CS1, D18):** a day lists the Sessions that start on it or whose game time covers it (its date associations, of a day, a month or festival, or a year), linked to the Sessions app through the registry's `session` link (a Session is only listed where the person may see it, and a failed read leaves the day without them); the header's bell is the account's unread count with the hub's page of them to go to; the status bar says which date is on show and whether anything is written on it ("Greening 22 · period written"); and the arrow keys move through the days of the month, along the week and a week up or down, with Page Up and Page Down changing the segment.
- **Where Calendars differs from the design on purpose, and why:** the arrows step the month, and the whole year's arrows the year, as the design's do, but the year's field stays beside them, since it is what reaches any year; an entry's panels that fold stay folded where the design draws them open (Order and causes is a card and, as drawn, a button in the head that opens it); the Events' time filter reads dates in the browser and not from the list ([#1135](https://github.com/ramsesoriginal/lorenzo/issues/1135)).
- **An Event says where it was presented (CS1):** a card "Where it was presented" lists the Sessions whose played Scenes presented it, and the Scene. The API reads a Session's play record and not an Event's, so each Session the person can see is asked; one whose record is not theirs to read is left out and nothing says it was there. Presented in a Scene is not order, cause or knowledge, and the card says so.
- **Appearance is reachable without an account:** the palette's "Change theme" (and G then T) opens the Appearance dialog, which it did not (the web header's toggle is a hidden placeholder), so a visitor who is not signed in can choose and the choice is kept.
- **The day panel is a sheet wherever it does not fit beside the grid (CS6):** below 1100px (a phone and the 834px tablet the design draws) the panel is fixed to the bottom of the window, not there until a date (or the year's notes) is chosen, and gone with Close, which puts the focus back on the day; from 1100px it is docked beside the grid.

## Checked against the design

A pass over the screens that belong to Calendars (`docs/design/web/calendars-sessions/screens/`), each compared with the page as built at 1440, 834 and 430 wide, and a pass over the app's README for what it could do before. What the pass found is in the slices above; this is where each screen stands.

The year's field, "Open year" and the buttons for the calendar's entry and for the year's and month's notes are kept in the head, where the design has only the arrows and Edit: they are what reaches any year and the notes that are not on a day |

Nothing the app did before is missing: browsing and the whole year, defining a calendar with its preview and its stale-definition review, a date's notes and entries, creating Events, Facts and observances on a day, the entry page and its audiences, Order and causes with sharing and Compare, Events with their filters and story order, participants, learning and groups, clocks, link names and the prefetching. What changed is where it is: appearance is in the account menu (and, without an account, the palette's Change theme).

## Consequences

- The e2e tests that drove the old header (the toggle, the "Account" link, "Log out") drive the account menu and the Appearance dialog instead.
- The pages' contents move to the design in the slices that follow, each its own pull request on the one before.
